Network Topology

Sioux Falls benchmark: 24 nodes, 76 directed links, 528 OD pairs. Road classification from actual city geography: 6 interstate links (I-29/I-229, 3 or 2 lanes, 105 km/h) and 70 arterial links (2 lanes, 30–70 km/h).

TNTP total demand: 360,600. Original paper values × 100 = 0.1 × daily ≈ hourly. TNTP ‘capacity’ column is a BPR math artifact (back-computed from polynomial coefficients), not physical road capacity.

Demand Scaling: Oversaturation

Number of links reaching jam density as demand increases. With realistic 2–3 lane roads, the network handles up to ~10% of TNTP demand before widespread congestion. At 100%, nearly all links gridlock — the TNTP benchmark was designed for BPR’s infinite-capacity math, not physical roads.

Demand Scaling: Speed

Network-average speed as fraction of free-flow. The MFD-based VDF imposes a physical capacity ceiling: once demand exceeds it, speed drops to the minimum (jam). Unlike BPR which degrades gracefully at V/C > 1, the bi-parabolic model correctly models gridlock.

Demand Scaling: TSTT

Total system travel time rises exponentially as demand approaches physical capacity, then explodes as links jam.

Convergence at 10% Demand

FW gap: 0.0198, MSA gap: 0.3271 (50 iterations). Demand: 36,060 vph (10% of TNTP).

Flow Correlation (10% Demand)

Spearman rank correlation: r = 0.132 (76 links). MFD flows are at 10% of TNTP demand while BPR reference is at 100%, so magnitudes differ by ~10×. The rank ordering tests whether both VDFs load the same links heavily.
